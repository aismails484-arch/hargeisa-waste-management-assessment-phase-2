# Technical Field Assessment: Hargeisa Waste Management (Phase 2)
**Project Title:** Urban Sanitation Resilience & Environmental Risk Mapping  
**Lead Researcher:** Ahmed Ismail Saed Hussein  
**Agency:** Ministry of Environment and Climate Change, Republic of Somaliland.

## 1. Executive Summary
Phase 2 of the Hargeisa Waste Management Assessment provides a data-driven evaluation of current sanitation infrastructure. This report focuses on mitigating environmental risks caused by improper waste disposal in the capital's natural drainage systems and public spaces.

## 2. Key Findings
* **Operational Capacity:** The current fleet and workforce are operating at 65% capacity due to logistical bottlenecks in the Macalin Haruun and Gacan Libaax sectors.
* **Volume Analysis:** Daily waste generation exceeds collection rates by 15-20%, leading to mass illegal dumping in natural waterways.
* **Community Engagement:** High willingness (82%) from local households to pay for reliable, scheduled waste collection services.

## 3. Major Gaps
* **Technical Deficit:** Lack of smart tracking (GPS) for collection vehicles, leading to inefficient routing.
* **Legal Enforcement:** Minimal implementation of environmental fines for industrial waste generators.
* **Infrastructure:** Absence of a secondary sorting facility (Recycling Hub) to reduce landfill pressure.

## 4. Risk Areas & Environmental Impact
* **Togga Hargeisa (Riparian Zone Pollution):** This is the highest risk zone. Waste dumped along the banks is carried by runoff during the rainy season, contaminating downstream ecosystems and groundwater aquifers.
* **Intra-City Drainage Channels (Laaggaha Xaafadaha):** Natural and man-made drainage channels passing through neighborhoods (e.g., 26-ka June and Ahmed Dhagax) have become informal dumping sites. This results in:
    * **Blockage & Flooding:** Stagnant water leading to flash floods during rainfall and mosquito-borne disease outbreaks.
    * **Structural Damage:** Accumulation of solid waste undermines the integrity of urban drainage infrastructure.
* **Public Markets:** Critical accumulation zones that require 24/7 collection cycles to prevent public health crises.

## 5. Service Analysis
The assessment indicates that the existing Private-Public Partnership (PPP) model requires a structural overhaul. Current service providers lack specialized equipment for hazardous and medical waste, which is frequently mixed with general household waste.

## 6. Strategic Recommendations
* **Digital Integration:** Deploy AI-driven route optimization to reduce fuel costs and increase coverage across the 'Laag' networks.
* **Decentralized Sorting:** Establish district-level "Mini-Hubs" for plastic and organic waste separation before they reach the Togga.
* **Policy Reform:** Institutionalize the National Waste Management Unit (NWMU) protocols to standardize service quality and enforce dumping bans.

## 7. Conclusion
Phase 2 confirms that Hargeisa’s sanitation crisis is rooted in logistical and regulatory challenges. By implementing this "Strategic Sanitation Roadmap," the Ministry can protect Hargeisa’s natural waterways (Togga & Laaggaha) and transform waste management into a sustainable economic sector.
